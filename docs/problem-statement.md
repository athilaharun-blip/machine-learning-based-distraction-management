# Part A: Problem Formulation

## A1. Problem Statement

### Who is affected?

University students who use digital devices for studying are affected by digital distractions, particularly when they have difficulty maintaining their attention during study activities.

### What exactly is going wrong?

Students may be distracted by digital activities such as social media, entertainment, messaging, notifications, and other non-academic activities while using digital devices. These distractions can interrupt their attention and make it difficult to maintain focus during study sessions. A cross-sectional study by Thapa et al. (2025) found that 51.2% of surveyed students used digital devices for non-classroom activities 1–3 times during a class, while 68.6% identified reduced attention as one of the main negative effects of non-academic digital device use.

### Why does it matter?

Digital distractions can interfere with students' ability to maintain focus and may reduce the effectiveness of their study activities. When a student's attention changes during a study session, it may be difficult to recognize when the student is becoming distracted and when additional support may be appropriate. Therefore, the lack of awareness of students' attention states and their surrounding context is an important problem in managing digital distractions during study activities.

### Problem Statement (3–5 sentences)

University students may experience digital distractions while using digital devices for studying, which can affect their ability to maintain focus. Digital activities such as social media, entertainment, messaging, and notifications may interrupt students' attention during study sessions. Thapa et al. (2025) reported that 51.2% of surveyed students used digital devices for non-classroom activities 1–3 times during class, while 68.6% reported reduced attention as a negative effect. This indicates that digital distraction is a relevant issue that can interfere with students' attention during learning activities. Therefore, the lack of awareness of students' attention states and their surrounding context makes digital distraction management more difficult.

### Evidence Source

## A2. Users and Scope

### Main Users

**University students**  
Students who use digital devices during study activities and may experience digital distractions. They are the main users affected by the problem and the intended users of the project.

### Stakeholders

- **University students** – primary users of the system.
- **Lecturers/educators** – stakeholders who are interested in supporting students' focus and learning activities.
- **Project stakeholders/supervisors** – stakeholders involved in evaluating the project and its outcomes.

### In Scope

The project will focus on:

1. Collecting relevant information about the student's current study context.
2. Classifying the student's attention state using machine learning.
3. Displaying the classified attention state to the student.
4. Identifying situations related to digital distraction based on the student's context and attention state.
5. Providing suitable context-aware support to help students manage digital distractions during study activities.

### Out of Scope

The project will not:

1. Diagnose medical, psychological, or attention-related disorders.
2. Treat or cure screen addiction or other medical conditions.
3. Replace professional counselling, psychological support, or medical services.
4. Monitor students secretly or without their knowledge.
5. Guarantee the complete elimination of digital distractions.
6. Evaluate or grade students' academic performance.
